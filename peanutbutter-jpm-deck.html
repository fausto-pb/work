Why we can, and why the incumbents won’t
03 / 06

We are not a better tool for a finance team. We are what a business buys when it doesn’t have one.

Accounting firms and independent accountants

Capacity is people, so revenue scales with headcount they cannot hire — the shortage creating our demand is the same shortage capping their supply. They are not adversaries: the close, the statements and the filings stay with them, and they get cleaner data than they’ve ever had.

The AP software companies

Ramp launched AI agents for accounts payable in October 2025 — coding, fraud detection, approval recommendations, card payments — and extended to bookkeeping in February 2026. BILL, Stampli and Vic.ai have layered agentic features through 2026. Take it seriously. Four reasons this revenue is not theirs to take:

01

They sell software someone operates. A person connects the accounting system, configures approval flows, reviews recommendations and clicks approve. Their agents recommend; a human decides. A business without a finance team has nobody to do any of that.

02

Their buyer is a finance team, and most of this market doesn’t have one. They sell to controllers and finance operations staff at companies sophisticated enough to run them — a real and valuable market, and a small fraction of the businesses that must pay invoices every month.

03

Their revenue model argues against it. Ramp’s autonomous payment action is card entry, because interchange is the revenue. Doing this work as labor means a fee for completing it regardless of how money moves — competing with their own economics rather than extending them. A company priced as software cannot deliberately move labor onto its cost of goods sold; that decision does not clear a board. A pricing constraint, not a capability one.

Why it could compound

Verification done once serves every subsequent customer. Every vendor verified for one business is already verified for the next — and every vendor we pay is itself a business with invoices to pay. A network that gets more valuable and cheaper to serve with each customer, available only to whoever does the work rather than sells the tool. An opportunity ahead of us, not a moat we hold today.

What was built

Identity. Every actor, human or AI, is cryptographically distinguishable and cannot claim to be another. Per-actor credentials, no shared keys, signature-based authentication.

Authority. What each actor may decide is enforced outside the AI itself, with hard ceilings. It cannot argue its way past a limit, because the limit isn’t in the instructions.

Communication. Work passes between parts of the system as structured, validated data rather than one AI instructing another. Every exchange becomes record.

Traceability. An append-only ledger, cryptographically hash-chained and anchored outside our own control, so an outsider can verify the record rather than take our word for it.

Verification. Work is checked by something other than what produced it. The doer never approves itself.

Accountability. Every action traces to a responsible human, and there is a kill switch.

The architectural ideas

The spine. One authoritative record every process writes to. Nothing happens outside it.

A closed-loop, queryable company. Our own operations are structured data, so the company can be asked questions and answer from evidence rather than from someone’s memory. Every human judgment is captured with its reasoning, and that class of exception stops needing a person.

Agent-to-agent interoperability. Built on emerging open standards rather than a proprietary island, so what we operate connects to what customers and partners run.

Standards adopted deliberately, not invented
OAuth 2.1 with token exchange for delegated authority · the current Model Context Protocol authorization profile for tool access · BIP-340 Schnorr signatures for actor identity, implemented and verified against the full official test-vector set and independently cross-checked · append-only Postgres with cryptographic hash chaining and external anchoring for the record · NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework as the documentation spine, with ISO/IEC 42001 and SOC 2 Type II as the attestation path · EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure applied everywhere by default.
Operating licenses

An operating license is an internal permission gating what the system may do at all. Three exist. Each stays unsigned until its preconditions are met, and the refusal is written into the source code rather than a setting someone can toggle.
