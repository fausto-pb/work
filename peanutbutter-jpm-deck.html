The work01 / 06

Every business pays invoices. Every month. Forever.

An invoice arrives. It has to be checked, matched, coded, approved, paid, and recorded.

That's the work. We do it — not software a business operates, the work itself, done and answered for.

What we do
  • Vendor invoices, any channel — checked against purchase orders, receiving records, contracts, or that vendor's history.
  • Vendor identity and payment details — maintained and verified, with a hard gate on any change to where money goes.
  • Recurring bills and subscriptions.
  • Contractor payments, with tax documentation collected for those we onboard.
  • Payment on terms, including early-payment discounts. Cash-priority decisions escalate to the owner.
  • The entry on their books. Close and statements stay with their accountant.

Procurement, payroll, tax filing and treasury are deliberately out of scope. That is a decision, not a gap.

Why now, and how big02 / 06

The technology is here. The labor is not.

AI can now do this work end to end rather than assist someone doing it: read any invoice format, match it against whatever evidence exists, code it, route it, pay it, record it. That is new, and it is why this company exists now rather than three years ago.

Why we can, and why the incumbents won't03 / 06

This revenue is not theirs to take.

Accounting firms and independent accountants

Capacity is people, so revenue scales with headcount they cannot hire — the shortage creating our demand is the same shortage capping their supply. They are not adversaries. The close, the statements and the filings stay with them, and they get cleaner data than they have ever had.

The accounts payable software companies

Ramp launched AI agents for accounts payable in October 2025 — coding, fraud detection, approval recommendations, card payments — and extended to bookkeeping in February 2026. BILL, Stampli and Vic.ai have layered agentic features through 2026. Four reasons the category does not absorb this:

  1. They sell software someone operates. A person connects the accounting system, configures approval flows and permissions, reviews recommendations and clicks approve. Their agents recommend; a human decides. A business without a finance team has nobody to do any of that. We are not a better tool for a finance team — we are what a business buys when it doesn't have one.
  2. Their buyer is a finance team, and most of this market doesn't have one. These products are sold to controllers and finance operations staff at companies sophisticated enough to run them. A real and valuable market, and a small fraction of the businesses that must pay invoices every month.
  3. Their revenue model argues against it. Ramp's autonomous payment action is card entry, because interchange is the revenue. Doing this work as labor means being paid a fee to complete the work regardless of how money moves. A company priced as software cannot deliberately move labor onto its cost of goods sold; that decision does not clear a board. A pricing constraint, not a capability one.
  4. Penetration is early. However fast agentic features ship, the share of US businesses whose payables actually run this way remains small — [penetration figure and source, or this claim comes off the slide].

What we built05 / 06

75 essential controls, all built and proven by tests that execute.

Verified through adversarial internal audit. There has been no third-party or independent audit. External attestation is the roadmap, not the record.

What was built
  • Identity. Every actor, human or AI, is cryptographically distinguishable and cannot claim to be another. Per-actor credentials, no shared keys, signature-based authentication.
  • Authority. What each actor may decide is enforced outside the AI itself, with hard ceilings. The system cannot argue its way past a limit, because the limit isn't in the instructions.
  • Communication. Work passes between parts of the system as structured, validated data rather than one AI instructing another. Every exchange becomes record.
  • Traceability. An append-only ledger, cryptographically hash-chained and anchored outside our own control, so an outsider can verify the record rather than take our word for it.
  • Verification. Work is checked by something other than what produced it. The doer never approves itself.
  • Accountability. Every action traces to a responsible human, and there is a kill switch.
The architectural ideas
  • The spine. One authoritative record every process writes to. Nothing happens outside it.
  • A closed-loop, queryable company. Our own operations are structured data, so the company can be asked questions and answer from evidence rather than from someone's memory. Every human judgment is captured with its reasoning, and that class of exception stops needing a person.
  • Agent-to-agent interoperability. Built on emerging open standards rather than a proprietary island, so what we operate connects to what customers and partners run.
Standards adopted, not invented
  • OAuth 2.1 with token exchange, for delegated authority.
  • The current Model Context Protocol authorization profile, for tool access.
  • BIP-340 Schnorr signatures for actor identity — implemented and verified against the full official test-vector set, and independently cross-checked.
  • Append-only Postgres with cryptographic hash chaining and external anchoring, for the record.
  • NIST's AI Risk Management Framework as the documentation spine, with ISO/IEC 42001 and SOC 2 Type II as the attestation path.
  • EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure, applied everywhere by default.
Operating licenses

An operating license is an internal permission gating what the system may do at all. Three exist:

  • Moving money.
  • Acting without human approval.
  • Sending communications unsupervised.

Each stays unsigned until its preconditions are met, and the refusal is written into the source code rather than a setting someone can toggle.
