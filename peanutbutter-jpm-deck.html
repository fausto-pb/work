The work

Open one step back

Every business pays invoices. Every month. Forever.

An invoice arrives. It has to be checked, matched, coded, approved, paid, and recorded.

That's the work. We do it — not software a business operates, the work itself, done and answered for.

The scope, said out loud
  • Vendor invoices, any channel — checked against purchase orders, receiving records, contracts, or that vendor's history.
  • Vendor identity and payment details — maintained and verified, with a hard gate on any change to where money goes.
  • Recurring bills and subscriptions — the internal list of what is due, kept current.
  • Contractor payments — with tax documentation collected for those we onboard.
  • Payment on terms — including early-payment discounts. Cash-priority decisions escalate to the owner.
  • The entry on their books — close and statements stay with their accountant.
The boundary, as a decision

Procurement, payroll, tax filing and treasury are deliberately out of scope. That is a decision, not a gap.

Language discipline

A vendor sends an invoice — that is the document. A business owes a payable. Bills are the internal list of what's due. Never mix them in one sentence.

Do not open with a category name, and do not open with AI. The transaction is the opening.

If asked "so what is it, software?"

No. A business buying software still has to staff someone to run it. They are buying the work done and answered for. We are the layer above the treasury stack their bank provides.

Why we can, and why the incumbents won't

Accounting firms and independent accountants

Capacity is people, so revenue scales with headcount they cannot hire — the shortage creating our demand is the same shortage capping their supply. They are not adversaries: the close, the statements and the filings stay with them, and they get cleaner data than they've ever had.

The AP software companies — take them seriously

Ramp launched AI agents for accounts payable in October 2025 — coding, fraud detection, approval recommendations, card payments — and extended to bookkeeping in February 2026. BILL, Stampli and Vic.ai have layered agentic features through 2026. Four reasons the category doesn't absorb this, and say all four:

1 · Software someone operates. They sell software someone operates. A person connects the accounting system, configures approval flows and permissions, reviews recommendations and clicks approve. Their agents recommend; a human decides. A business without a finance team has nobody to do any of that. We are not a better tool for a finance team — we are what a business buys when it doesn't have one.

2 · Buyer is a finance team. Their buyer is a finance team, and most of this market doesn't have one. These products are sold to controllers and finance operations staff at companies sophisticated enough to run them. A real and valuable market, and a small fraction of the businesses that must pay invoices every month.

3 · Interchange, not labor. Their revenue model argues against it. Ramp's autonomous payment action is card entry, because interchange is the revenue. Doing this work as labor means being paid a fee to complete the work regardless of how money moves — competing with their own economics rather than extending them. A company priced as software cannot deliberately move labor onto its cost of goods sold; that decision does not clear a board. A pricing constraint, not a capability one.

4 · Penetration still early. However fast agentic features ship, the share of US businesses whose payables actually run this way remains small. [No penetration figure shown without a source.]

Exhibit 2 — the economics, four models
  • Services revenue with human costs — the accounting firm: high revenue per customer, cost of goods sold is salaries, margin where services businesses actually land.
  • Software revenue with software costs — SaaS: low revenue per customer relative to the function's total cost, high margin.
  • Services revenue with software costs — this company: the revenue of the first with the cost structure of the second.
  • The same, over time — the arrow: cost per unit of work falls as the system learns, while price holds.

Industry margin ranges on this chart are illustrative and directional, not sourced figures, and not drawn to scale. Our own position is a bracket rather than a point because we are pre-revenue and have measured no margin of our own. Direction, not data. Do not let the chart imply otherwise.

Software historically captured a small fraction of what a business function costs; the rest was the payroll of the people operating it. Selling the work invoices against that larger line while carrying software costs. We instrument cost per unit of work from the first transaction, so this becomes a measurement rather than a claim.

The compounding opportunity — opportunity, not a moat we hold

Verification done once serves every subsequent customer. Every vendor verified for one business is already verified for the next — and every vendor we pay is itself a business with invoices to pay. A network that gets more valuable and cheaper to serve with each customer, available only to whoever does the work rather than sells the tool.

What we built — go deeper here than anywhere else

The number, and what stands behind it

75 essential controls. All 75 built and proven by tests that execute rather than by assertion. Verified through adversarial internal audit — roughly 40 attack attempts against the money path, every one refused.

Say this plainly and unprompted: there has been no third-party or independent audit. External attestation is the roadmap, not the record. Do not claim otherwise, and do not let a question imply it.

The six areas, one line each
  • Identity — every actor, human or AI, is cryptographically distinguishable and cannot claim to be another. Per-actor credentials, no shared keys, signature-based authentication.
  • Authority — what each actor may decide is enforced outside the AI itself, with hard ceilings. The system cannot argue its way past a limit, because the limit isn't in the instructions.
  • Communication — work passes between parts of the system as structured, validated data rather than one AI instructing another; every exchange becomes record.
  • Traceability — an append-only ledger, cryptographically hash-chained and anchored outside our own control, so an outsider can verify the record rather than take our word for it.
  • Verification — work is checked by something other than what produced it; the doer never approves itself.
  • Accountability — every action traces to a responsible human, and there is a kill switch.
The architectural ideas, named plainly
  • The spine — one authoritative record every process writes to. Nothing happens outside it.
  • A closed-loop, queryable company — the company's own operations are structured data, so it can be asked questions and answer from evidence rather than from someone's memory. Every human judgment is captured with its reasoning, and that class of exception stops needing a person.
  • Agent-to-agent interoperability — built on emerging open standards rather than a proprietary island, so what we operate connects to what customers and partners run.
Standards adopted deliberately rather than invented

OAuth 2.1 with token exchange for delegated authority; the current Model Context Protocol authorization profile for tool access; BIP-340 Schnorr signatures for actor identity, implemented and verified against the full official test-vector set and independently cross-checked; append-only Postgres with cryptographic hash chaining and external anchoring for the record; NIST's AI Risk Management Framework as the documentation spine, with ISO/IEC 42001 and SOC 2 Type II as the attestation path; EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure applied everywhere by default.

Operating licenses — define the phrase, it means nothing without it

An operating license is an internal permission gating what the system may do at all. Three exist: moving money; acting without human approval; sending communications unsupervised. Each stays unsigned until its preconditions are met, and the refusal is written into the source code rather than a setting someone can toggle.
