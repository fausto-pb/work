01 · The workSpeaker notes

Open one step back

Every business pays invoices. Every month. Forever.

Say that first and let it sit. It is simply true, it costs nothing to agree with, and it puts the room in the right frame before a single word about the company.

Then the sentence on the slide

An invoice arrives. It has to be checked, matched, coded, approved, paid, and recorded.

The invoice is the subject of that sentence, not a person. That is deliberate. The work is a property of the transaction, not of whoever happens to be doing it. Do not rewrite it as “somebody has to handle it” — the whole argument later depends on the work existing independently of the person.

That's the work. We do it — not software a business operates, the work itself, done and answered for.

“Done and answered for” is the phrase to land. It is the difference between a tool and a provider, and it is what makes the controls slide matter later.

Never open with the category

Do not say “we do financial operations.” In a real meeting a banker heard that as finance — investments, factoring, treasury — and the rest of the conversation was spent climbing back. Name the transaction, not the category. Do not open with AI either; that comes on slide two, as the reason this is possible now.

The scope, in full

  • Vendor invoices, any channel. Checked against purchase orders, receiving records, contracts, or that vendor's history — whatever evidence exists for that business.
  • Vendor identity and payment details. Maintained and verified, with a hard gate on any change to where money goes. This is the single most common fraud vector in payables and it is worth pausing on if anyone in the room reacts to it.
  • Recurring bills and subscriptions.
  • Contractor payments, with tax documentation collected for those we onboard.
  • Payment on terms, including early-payment discounts. Cash-priority decisions escalate to the owner — we do not decide who gets paid when money is tight.
  • The entry on their books. Close and statements stay with their accountant.

The boundary, said as a decision

Procurement, payroll, tax filing and treasury are deliberately out of scope. That is a decision, not a gap.

Treasury being out of scope is the line that matters in this room. We operate the layer above the treasury stack a bank provides. A customer of ours moves more volume through their bank, not less.

Language discipline

A vendor sends an invoice — the document. A business owes a payable. Bills are the internal list of what's due. Never mix the three in one sentence; it reads as someone who has not done the work.

03 · Why we can, and why the incumbents won'tSpeaker notes

First: accounting firms and independent accountants

Capacity is people, so their revenue scales with headcount they cannot hire. The shortage creating our demand is the same shortage capping their supply.

They are not adversaries, and say so out loud. The close, the statements and the filings stay with them, and they get cleaner data than they have ever had. This matters in this room: bankers know accountants, and a pitch that sounds like it is at war with the accounting profession lands badly.

Then: take the AP software companies seriously

Ramp launched AI agents for accounts payable in October 2025 — coding, fraud detection, approval recommendations, card payments — and extended to bookkeeping in February 2026. BILL, Stampli and Vic.ai have layered agentic features through 2026. Name them before the room does. The argument is structural, not dismissive.

1 — They sell software someone operates

A person connects the accounting system, configures approval flows and permissions, reviews recommendations and clicks approve. Their agents recommend; a human decides. A business without a finance team has nobody to do any of that.

We are not a better tool for a finance team. We are what a business buys when it doesn't have one.

2 — Their buyer is a finance team, and most of this market doesn't have one

These products are sold to controllers and finance operations staff at companies sophisticated enough to run them. That is a real and valuable market. It is also a small fraction of the businesses that must pay invoices every month.

3 — Their revenue model argues against it

Ramp's autonomous payment action is card entry, because interchange is the revenue. Doing this work as labor means being paid a fee to complete the work regardless of how money moves — competing with their own economics rather than extending them.

A company priced as software cannot deliberately move labor onto its cost of goods sold. That decision does not clear a board. This is a pricing constraint, not a capability one, and that distinction is the whole point — do not claim they can't build it.

4 — Penetration is early

However fast agentic features ship, the share of US businesses whose payables actually run this way remains small.

06 · What we builtSpeaker notes

[Fausto: verify every technical claim on this slide against the current build before this is shown to anyone.]

The headline number

75 essential controls. All 75 are built and proven by tests that execute, not by assertion. Verified through adversarial internal audit — roughly 40 attack attempts against the money path, every one refused.

Do not claim third-party or independent audit. There has been none. External attestation is the roadmap, not the record. Say that before anyone asks; in this room, volunteering the limit buys more credit than the claim would.

The six areas, one line each

  • Identity. Every actor, human or AI, is cryptographically distinguishable and cannot claim to be another. Per-actor credentials, no shared keys, signature-based authentication.
  • Authority. What each actor may decide is enforced outside the AI itself, with hard ceilings. The system cannot argue its way past a limit, because the limit isn't in the instructions.
  • Communication. Work passes between parts of the system as structured, validated data rather than one AI instructing another. Every exchange becomes record.
  • Traceability. An append-only ledger, cryptographically hash-chained and anchored outside our own control, so an outsider can verify the record rather than take our word for it.
  • Verification. Work is checked by something other than what produced it. The doer never approves itself.
  • Accountability. Every action traces to a responsible human, and there is a kill switch.

The architectural ideas, named plainly

  • The spine. One authoritative record every process writes to. Nothing happens outside it.
  • A closed-loop, queryable company. Our own operations are structured data, so the company can be asked questions and answer from evidence rather than from someone's memory. Every human judgment is captured with its reasoning, and that class of exception stops needing a person.
  • Agent-to-agent interoperability. Built on emerging open standards rather than a proprietary island, so what we operate connects to what customers and partners run.

Standards adopted deliberately rather than invented

  • OAuth 2.1 with token exchange, for delegated authority.
  • The current Model Context Protocol authorization profile, for tool access.
  • BIP-340 Schnorr signatures for actor identity — implemented and verified against the full official test-vector set, and independently cross-checked.
  • Append-only Postgres with cryptographic hash chaining and external anchoring, for the record.
  • NIST's AI Risk Management Framework as the documentation spine, with ISO/IEC 42001 and SOC 2 Type II as the attestation path.
  • EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure, applied everywhere by default.

This is the deepest part of the deck. It is still business English — no code, no architecture diagrams. Go this deep only as far as the room stays with you, but be ready for all of it: a technical person reading over the banker's shoulder should recognise it as real.

Define “operating license” — the phrase means nothing without it

An operating license is an internal permission gating what the system may do at all. Three exist:

  • Moving money.
  • Acting without human approval.
  • Sending communications unsupervised.

Each stays unsigned until its preconditions are met, and the refusal is written into the source code rather than a setting someone can toggle.
