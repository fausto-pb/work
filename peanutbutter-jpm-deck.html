Businesses are run by fewer people every year. The finance function is the last one still done by hand.

The United States has been losing accountants faster than it replaces them. The direction has held for years and nothing in the pipeline reverses it.

A business without a finance department feels it first. Invoices sit. Work orders queue. The month closes late, and someone senior spends their evening in a spreadsheet reconstructing what the business already did.

This is an operations problem before it is a technology problem.

The spine
02 / 06

Two decisions of different natures.

Judgment stays human. Execution becomes machine, inside limits it cannot exceed.

There are three ways to fund a business: equity, debt, and revenue from operations. Which one to take is judgment. It depends on the owner, the moment, and what the business is for — and it is not a call a machine can make, or should be asked to.

Once the call is made, everything after it is execution. Getting the money. Moving it. Putting it to work. Proving where it went. Building software used to be that kind of work too, and it stopped being done by hand.

Where those two meet is the company. We built ourselves that way before we sold it to anyone.

What we run
03 / 06

Financial operations first. The operation is what earns the credit layer.

01
We run the operation

Accounts payable, work orders, procurement — the money movements that keep a business running. Payroll and tax are deliberately out of scope for now. Our customer is any business hiring for accounting or outsourcing it, and they are not choosing between us and software. They are choosing between us and a hire they cannot make.

02
The operation earns the credit layer

Running those operations means holding a business's payables calendar and its cash cycle — what it owes, to whom, when, and whether it can cover it. That makes us the party positioned to originate credit at the moment of need, through banking partners, with use of proceeds already instrumented. The lender watches the money work rather than reconstructing it from statements at quarter end. The money never leaves the rail. The balance sheet is the bank's; we are the layer that makes business credit deployable and observable.

Operating is the depth. Distribution is the aperture. Operating produces distribution, not the reverse, and that order is the whole argument. A bank today is infrastructure for business, and it does not behave like one.

The economics

Software historically captured a small fraction of what a business function costs. The rest was payroll — the people operating the software. Selling the work rather than the tool means invoicing against that larger line.

A services business normally carries services economics: revenue scales with headcount. Ours does not. The work is done by the machine, and people build capability the machine does not yet have — never capacity. Every judgment a human makes is captured, and that class of exception stops needing a person.

Cost per unit of work falls as the system learns while price holds. We instrument cost per unit of work from the first transaction, so gross margin is a measurement rather than an assertion.

How it is built
04 / 06

The part a competitor cannot borrow.

Method

Nine primary sources on AI-native organisational design — read in full, stored in the repository, argued with in writing, and cited as the basis of the design rather than as inspiration. Every architectural decision is a numbered record carrying the options considered, the recommendation, and the specific evidence that would prove it wrong. 44 such records to date.

Architecture

The work is done by identified agents running written procedures over one ledger of facts, inside a boundary they cannot talk their way out of. Append-only event spine, hash chain computed inside the insert transaction. Nightly anchoring of the chain head to write-once storage in a separate account under compliance-mode object lock, and to a public transparency log we do not control — so an outsider can verify our record rather than trust it. Dual-layer enforcement: every rule is a check in the application and a constraint in the database, and a test proves it fails closed. Deterministic orchestration: no model decides routing, authority tier, ceiling or grant scope. Per-run brokered credentials with short lifetimes; raw secrets never enter an agent's context. Fresh-context independent verification, structurally separate from the party that did the work. Runtime egress control and hash-pinned tool definitions. Anything reading untrusted input — invoices, emails, vendor messages — is assumed compromisable, so nothing important depends on the model behaving.

Standards

37 external standards, specifications and regulations tracked with version, date checked and a re-check date, with a build gate that fails when a citation ages out or an uncited one appears. Among them: Model Context Protocol and A2A for agent interoperability; OAuth 2.1 with RFC 8693 token exchange for delegated authority; Sigstore Rekor and in-toto for provenance; OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions; Cedar and OPA for policy; NIST AI RMF; ISO/IEC 42001 with 42006 accreditation; SOC 2 Type II; EU AI Act Article 50 transparency; GDPR; OWASP Agentic Top 10; MITRE ATLAS. Most companies name standards in a table. We store them, version them, and fail the build when one moves.

Proof of rigour

1,014 tests execute on every change. 402 mutation entries: each safety rule is deleted on a throwaway database and the suite must go red — a rule nobody notices missing was never a rule. 91 database migrations. 116 open defects on a standing register, each with a named owner. Three independent audits commissioned this month.
